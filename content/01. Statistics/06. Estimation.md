## Introduction
- The Purpose of Statistics is to use the information contained **in a sample to make inferences about the population** from which the sample is taken.
- 우리는 샘플로부터 모집단을 잘 추론하는 것이 통계학의 목적이다.
- We will call the parameter of interest in the experiment the target parameter
	- First, we could use a single number, like Population mean $\mu$. $\to$ Point Estimate
	- Second, we might say that $\mu$ will fall between two numbers $\to$ Interval Estimate

**DEFINITION 8.1**
- An $\text{estimator}$ is a rule, often expressed as a formula, that tells how to calculate the value of an estimate based on the measurements contained in a sample
- For example, the Sample mean 
$$\bar{Y} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_iY_i$$ is one possible point estimator of the population mean $\mu$

## The Bias and Mean Square Error of Point Estimators
- Bias와 Unbiased의 개념은 추정량(Estimator)과 모수(Parameter) 사이 관계로 인해 정의된다.
1. 우리가 알고싶은 Parameter가 있다. 이를 $\theta$로 표기했을 때
2. 이 Parameter를 추정하기 위해 사용되는 함수가 Estimator(추정량)이다. 보통 $\hat{\theta}$로 표기됨.

- 여기에서 Bias는 다음과 같의 정의됨.
$$\text{Bias}(\hat\theta) = E(\hat\theta)-\theta$$
- $Bisa(\hat\theta) = 0$ 이라면, 즉 $E(\hat\theta) = \theta$라면 우리는 Unbiased(비편향)이라고 한다. 

**DEFINITION 8.2**
- Let $\hat\theta$ be a point estimator for a parameter $\theta$. Then $\hat\theta$ is an unbiased estimator if $E(\hat\theta) = \theta$. If $E(\hat\theta) \neq \theta$, $\hat\theta$ is said to be biased.

**DEFINITION 8.3**
- The bias of a point estimator $\hat\theta$ is given by $B(\hat\theta) = E(\hat\theta) - \theta$

**DEFINITION 8.4**
- The mean square error of a point estimator $\hat\theta$ is 
$$MSE(\hat\theta) = E[(\hat\theta-\theta)^2]$$ The mean square error of an estimator $\hat\theta$, $MSE(\hat\theta)$, is a function of both its **variance** and its **bias**.
$$MSE(\hat\theta) = V(\hat\theta) + [B(\hat\theta)]^2$$
- We often seek unbiased estimators with relatively small variances
- Unbiased하고 분산이 작은 것이 좋은 추정 값이다.![[Pasted image 20241202105137.png]]
## Some Common Unbiased Point Estimators
- 우리는 다음 장에서 sample mean $\bar{Y}$를 통해 Population mean $\mu$를 추정하고, Sample Proportion $\hat p = Y/n$을 통해 binomial parameter $p$ 를 추정하는 것이 자연스럽게 될 것이다.
- If an inference is to be based on independent random samples of $n_1$ and $n_2$ observations selected from two different populations, how would we estimate the difference between means $\mu_1 - \mu_2$ or the difference in two binomial parameters $p_1 - p_2$?![[Pasted image 20241202105919.png]]
- 우리는 Sample Variance를 계산할 때
	- $s^2 = \frac{1}{n-1}\sum(Y_i-\bar{Y})^2$ : Unbaised
	- $s^{`2} = \frac{1}{n}\sum(Y_1-\bar{Y})^2$ : Bias
**Proof**
$$\sum_i(Y_i - \bar{Y})^2 = \sum_iY_i^2 - \frac{1}{n}\left(\sum_iY_i\right)^2 = \sum_i Y_i^2 - n\bar{Y}^2$$
Hence
$$E\left[\sum_i(Y_i-\bar{Y})^2\right] = E\left(\sum_iY_i^2\right) - nE(\bar{Y}^2) = \sum_iE(Y_i^2) - nE(\bar{Y}^2)$$
Notice that $E(Y_i^2)$ is the same for $i=1, 2, \dots, n$. We use this and the fact that the variance of a random variable is given by $V(Y) = E(Y^2) - [E(Y)]^2$ to conclude that $E(Y_i^2) = V(Y_i) + [E(Y_i)]^2 = \sigma^2 + \mu^2$
$E(\bar{Y}^2) = V(\bar{Y}) + [E(\bar{Y})]^2 = \sigma^2/n +\mu^2$, and that 
$$\begin{align}E\left[\sum_i(Y_i-\bar{Y})^2\right] &= \sum_i(\sigma^2+\mu^2) - n\left(\frac{\sigma^2}{n} + \mu^2\right) \\ &= n(\sigma^2 + \mu^2) - n\left(\frac{\sigma^2}{n} + \mu^2\right) \\ &=n\sigma^2-\sigma^2 \\ &= (n-1)\sigma^2 \end{align}$$
- So, $s^2$ is an unbiased estimator for $\sigma^2$ 

## Evaluating the Goodness of a Point Estimator
- One way to measure the Goodness of any point estimation procedure is in terms of the ==distance between the estimates that it generates and the target parameter.==
- This quantity is called the **Error of Estimation**

**DEFINITION 8.5**
- The Error of Estimation $\epsilon$ is the distance between an estimator and its target parameter. That is, $\epsilon = |\hat\theta - \theta|$.
